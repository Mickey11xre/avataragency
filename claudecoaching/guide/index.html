Your Plan & the Desktop App

Why the plan matters

Nearly everything in this guide runs on a paid plan. The free tier exists to let you try Claude — its usage limits are far too small to carry real work, and you'll hit them mid-task.

PlanWhat it means for you
FreeBasic chat with limited usage. Fine for testing; not for running your business on.
Pro — the program floorMuch higher usage, unlimited projects, Claude Code, Microsoft 365 integration. This is what your setup assumes.
MaxFive times Pro's usage (a twenty-times tier sits above it). For people running Claude all day, every day. Don't buy this yet.
TeamWhen you want staff in it too — admin controls, single sign-on, and your data isn't used for training by default.

The desktop app

Claude in a browser tab is a visitor. The desktop app makes it a coworker — it's where connectors and local integrations live, and it's one keystroke away instead of buried in twenty tabs.

Connectors

This is the difference between AI as a search box and AI wired into your actual work. Connectors let Claude read your real email, calendar, and files.

How they work day to day

You don't "run" a connector. You just ask Claude something that needs your data — "what's on my plate this week?" — and it works out which tools it needs. Every action Claude takes on your behalf requires your explicit approval. Its answers come back with citations showing which emails, events, or documents it used, linked to the originals.

Shot 02 A Claude answer showing source citations A response drawn from Gmail or Calendar with the citation chips visible. Ideally the "what's on my plate this week?" answer.

Claude citing the real emails and events behind its answer.

Adding a file from Google Drive to a chat

  1. Click the plus sign in the chat interface.
  2. Select "Add from Google Drive."
  3. Search your recent documents, or paste the document's URL.
  4. Send your message — Claude reads the document to inform its response.

You can add several files to one chat, as long as they fit the conversation's context window. Google Docs sync live from Drive, so you're always working from the current version.

Turning individual connectors on and off

  1. Click the plus sign in the chat interface.
  2. Hover over "Connectors."
  3. Toggle each connector on or off.
Shot 03 The Connectors toggle list Plus sign clicked, hovering "Connectors," showing the list with toggles. Crop tight to the menu.

Turning individual connectors on and off.

If a connector stops working

Reconnect your Google account rather than fighting it — this is the single most common cause of "Claude can't see my email anymore." Full steps are in Section 10.

Projects — Your Second Brain

Chats come and go. A project is where Claude keeps what it knows about your business permanently.

Creating a project

  1. Find Projects in the left navigation panel.
  2. Click the "+" button — you'll see the ways to create one.
  3. Choose "Start from scratch" to set up a new folder with its own instructions and files.

Projects are available on every plan. Free accounts are capped at five; paid plans are unlimited.

Shot 04 The Projects panel with the "+" button Left navigation showing Projects, with the + visible. Include your own project in the list if you have one.

Where projects live in the left navigation.

Adding your knowledge

Open your project, find the Files section, click "+", and either upload from your computer or choose "Drive" to pull straight from Google Drive. Everything you add becomes background knowledge for every chat inside that project.

The Drive option is available for private projects only — it's disabled on shared projects.

What to feed it

Services and pricing. Your FAQ. Two or three things you wrote, so Claude learns your voice. Process docs and templates. Rough is fine — Claude reads messy. Never upload customer names, health details, or account numbers.

Why this compounds

Every document you add makes every future answer better. A project you feed for three months is worth dramatically more than the day you built it — which is exactly why we set it up first.

Instructions — Teaching Claude Your Voice

Two layers: one that applies everywhere, one that applies inside a single project.

Instructions for Claude (account-wide)

These apply to every conversation you have.

  1. Click your initials in the lower-left corner.
  2. Select "Settings."
  3. Under "Instructions for Claude," describe how you want it to work with you — your preferred approaches, terms you use, how you like to be communicated with.
Shot 05 Settings → Instructions for Claude The Instructions for Claude field, ideally with real text in it. Blur anything private before sending.

Account-wide instructions apply to every chat.

Project instructions

These apply only inside one project — which is where your business voice belongs. This is the skeleton we used in your session:

Project instruction patternYou are the operations assistant for [business], a [type] in [city]. Voice: [two or three adjectives drawn from how you actually write]. Sign-offs: [your normal closing]. Always: [your rules — e.g. offer two time slots, never promise same-day]. Never: [your no-go list].
The calibration trick

Paste in a real message you wrote, then ask: "Rewrite this in my voice from the project instructions — then tell me what you changed and why." Tune the instructions until the rewrite sounds like you. Ten minutes here is worth more than a hundred clever prompts later.

Memory

Memory is what stops you re-explaining yourself every Monday.

Turning it on

  1. Go to Settings → Memory.
  2. Turn on "Generate memory from chats."
Shot 06 Settings → Memory, toggle on The Memory settings screen with "Generate memory from chats" switched on.

Memory carries context between conversations.

What it keeps

  • Your role, your projects, your professional context
  • How you like to communicate and work
  • Ongoing work and project details

What it doesn't

Incognito chats. Outside a project you'll see a ghost icon in the upper-right corner. Switch it on and that conversation isn't saved to memory or your chat history — useful for anything sensitive. Close it when you want Claude remembering again.

Turning it off

Two options, and the difference matters. Pause keeps existing memories but stops making new ones. Reset permanently deletes everything, including project memories — it cannot be undone.

Reset is forever

If you're troubleshooting, pause first. Reset only when you genuinely want to start from nothing.

Capability

Skills

A project is what Claude knows. A skill is how Claude does something — a repeatable procedure it loads when the moment calls for it.

Turning skills on

  1. Go to Settings → Capabilities and make sure "Code execution and file creation" is enabled. Skills won't work without it.
  2. Go to Customize → Skills.
  3. Toggle individual skills on or off.
Shot 07 Customize → Skills, with the toggle list The Skills page showing available skills and their on/off toggles.

Enabling skills.

The ones you get for free

Anthropic maintains built-in skills for Excel spreadsheets, Word documents, PowerPoint decks, and PDFs. You don't invoke them — with code execution on, Claude reaches for them when they fit. Ask for "a PowerPoint about Q3" and it uses the PowerPoint skill on its own.

Adding a custom skill

  1. Build the skill following the standard structure, then package the folder as a ZIP file.
  2. Go to Customize → Skills.
  3. Click the "+" button, then "+ Create skill."
  4. Select "Upload a skill" and choose your ZIP.
  5. It appears in your list, ready to toggle on.

Skills you upload are private to your account. On Team and Enterprise plans, an owner can provision skills for everyone.

Skill, project, or connector?

Connector = access to a service. Project = background knowledge always loaded. Skill = a procedure that activates when relevant, anywhere in Claude. They stack — a connector gives Claude the tool, a skill teaches it how to use the tool well.

Claude in Chrome

Claude alongside the web page you're actually looking at — reading it, clicking, filling forms while you watch.

Installing the extension

  1. Open a Google Chrome window.
    • Chrome only — not other Chromium browsers, not mobile.
  2. Visit the Chrome Web Store and find Claude in Chrome.
  3. Click "Add to Chrome."
  4. Sign in with your Claude account when prompted.
  5. Pin it: click the puzzle-piece icon, then the thumbtack next to "Claude."
  6. Grant the permissions it asks for.
Shot 08 The Claude side panel open next to a web page Chrome with the Claude side panel visible alongside a normal page. Use a neutral site, nothing private.

The side panel travels with you as you browse.

Connecting it to the desktop app

So Claude can drive the browser from a desktop chat:

  1. Click your initials (lower-left) → Settings.
  2. Go to Connectors.
  3. Find Claude in Chrome and click "Configure."
  4. Toggle it on, and install the extension if you haven't.

Once connected, Claude in Chrome appears in the Connectors dropdown on your chats. It's off by default — enable it per conversation.

Read this before you use it

Claude in Chrome acts on websites on your behalf. It has safety classifiers, but it's genuinely more powerful — and therefore riskier — than a chat window. Watch what it does the first few times, and never let it act on a page you wouldn't hand to an assistant.

The Prompt Patterns That Always Work

You don't need to be clever. You need to be specific about four things: the source, the job, the format, and the voice.

The morning brief

Every Monday, or every dayLook at my calendar and inbox for this week. Give me: my appointments, the follow-ups I owe people, anything overdue, and my top 3 priorities — then tell me which free hour to use for the biggest one.

The draft-in-my-voice

Any writing task, inside your projectDraft a reply to this in my voice, using the project instructions. Keep it under [length]. Offer two specific times. Don't promise anything I haven't approved.

The triage

Inbox, leads, requestsRead my unread messages from the last three days. Flag the ones only I can answer and tell me why. Draft replies to the routine ones in my voice — I'll review before anything sends.

The critique

When you want better, not just fasterHere's my [draft / plan / price list]. Tell me the three weakest things about it and what you'd change. Be direct — I'd rather hear it from you than from a customer.

The four ingredients

IngredientWhat it sounds like
Source"From my calendar and inbox…" / "Using the pricing doc in this project…"
Job"Draft…" "Summarize…" "Flag…" "Rank by…"
Format"Three bullets." "Under 100 words." "A table with two columns."
Voice & limits"In my voice." "Don't promise same-day." "Ask me before sending."
The first draft is never the system

When output isn't right, don't rewrite from scratch — tell Claude what was wrong and ask it to adjust. Then save the improved prompt into your project. That's how a good prompt becomes a permanent asset.

When Things Break

What you seeWhat to do
"Claude can't see my email / calendar anymore"Reconnect your Google account. This is the most common failure by a wide margin — tokens expire. Settings → Connectors → reconnect, and re-approve every permission screen.
Answers feel genericYou're probably chatting outside your project. Open the project first — that's where your knowledge and voice live.
It doesn't sound like youYour project instructions need tuning, not your prompt. Run the calibration trick in Section 5.
Skills aren't appearing"Code execution and file creation" is off. Settings → Capabilities.
You hit a usage limitYou're on Free, or doing genuinely heavy work on Pro. Check Section 2 before upgrading — most people don't need Max.
Claude got something wrongGood — that's the system working. It drafts, you approve. Never send anything you haven't read.
You're stuck and it's day fourOpen your twin coach. Ten minutes a day, thirty days, and it knows what we built.

The rule that prevents most problems

Claude drafts. You approve. Every automation we built keeps you in the loop before anything reaches a customer — that's deliberate, and you should keep it that way even as you get faster.
