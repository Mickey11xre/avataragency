The Community College Thriving Quotient™ (TQ) is a reliable (α = .92), valid instrument that was developed to measure the academic, social, and psychological aspects of undergraduate students’ experience in the community college environment that are most predictive of academic success, institutional fit, satisfaction with college, and ultimately graduation. The 24 items on the TQ cluster onto five scales:
A measure of the degree to which students are meaningfully processing what happens in class and energized by what they are learning.
I feel energized by the ideas I am learning in most of my classes.
A measure of students’ goal-directedness, investment of effort, and regulation of their own learning and use of time.
I am confident I will reach my educational goals.
A measure of students’ optimism and explanatory style.
I look for the best in situations, even when things seem hopeless.
A measure of students’ involvement in healthy relationships and social support networks, whether on or off campus.
I feel like my friends really care about me.
A measure of students’ desire to make a difference in the community around them, as well as their openness to differences in others.
I value interacting with people whose viewpoints are different from my own.
Pathways to thriving
Examining thriving among community college students developed out of a desire to understand student success more holistically than previous higher education research that had focused solely on student retention. Dr. Amy Azul revised the Thriving Quotient developed by Dr. Laurie Schreiner to fit the community college student’s experience. The items that measure thriving are the same, but the pathways predictive of thriving in the community college setting are different. The Thriving Quotient™ (TQ) uses concepts from positive psychology (i.e., flourishing) and Bean and Eaton’s (2000) retention model to assess thriving, defined as “the experiences of college students who are fully engaged intellectually, socially, and emotionally” (Schreiner, 2010, p. 4). Thriving is not about personality traits, but rather describes malleable qualities within a student, meaning that institutions can teach students to thrive. Dr. Azul’s research has documented specific experiences that serve as significant pathways to student thriving in the community college environment; these pathways are also measured in the online survey.
