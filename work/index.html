Paul Small My Hermosa Beach

A neighborhood film that shows buyers what it actually feels like to live in Hermosa Beach — with Paul as their guide, and without a single day on set.

  • Cinematic film
  • Digital clone
  • Vertical shorts
  • Storyboard
Client
Paul Small, real estate advisor with Compass, "The Art of Representation"
Market
West Los Angeles and the South Bay
Capture

Delivered
"My Hermosa Beach" (1:49) and his first neighborhood film, "Mar Vista" (1:32), with three vertical shorts
My Hermosa Beach · a "My Los Angeles" episodeApproved by Paul as delivered

The client

Paul Small is a native Angeleno, a former talent agent and a Certified Senior Advisor with Compass. He didn't want another listing video. He wanted a film that sells the neighborhood itself, the way a local would show it to a friend.

The capture

It started with one cloning session: 47 reference photographs of Paul from every angle — full body, close-ups, three-quarter and mid-speech — and his own voice. From that set we built a private likeness model, so Paul could walk the Strand, the Greenbelt and Pier Avenue without a camera crew in sight.

The craft

We wrote and storyboarded the film in four acts — who Hermosa is for, its micro-neighborhoods, the local businesses and the pier — and Paul signed off on every frame before production. Real photographs of the homes, storefronts and rooftop views anchor each scene, relit for golden hour, with real-geography aerials carrying the camera between them. His on-camera lines are lip-synced exactly, then cut, graded and mixed in a professional edit.

The Thriving Project · homepage hero filmLive on thrivingincollege.org

The client

Dr. Laurie Schreiner created the Thriving Quotient, a research-based measure of how college students flourish — intellectually, socially and psychologically. Her old website didn't reflect decades of scholarship. She wanted a home for the Thriving Project that felt alive, credible and unmistakably hers.

The capture

We captured Laurie on site in Upland, California: reference stills from every angle and two long talking takes in 4K. She then recorded the narration herself, so the film carries her real voice from the first word to the last.

The craft

The website is a scroll-driven story: the page grows as visitors move through it, leading them from what it means to thrive to the research, the instruments and the store. The film places Laurie in her home office, a lecture hall, a library and a morning on campus, intercut with the students her work is for.

Danny Gomes Legacy Realty Partners

Three films and a set of social shorts for an agent whose clients are often settling a loved one's estate — calm, warm and steady, a guide rather than a salesman.

  • Cinematic film
  • Digital clone
  • Vertical shorts

Danny Gomes, Legacy Realty Partners — probate, trust and conservatorship sales
Likeness
Three approved looks, refined into five clean references; real photographs of his office as sets
Voice

Delivered
Agent Recruiting (1:19), Probate Outreach (1:11), Brand Story (1:20), two captioned vertical shorts
Agent Recruiting · filmed in his real office1 of 3 films

The client

Danny Gomes helps families sell real estate through probate, trusts and conservatorships — some of the most delicate sales in the business. He needed films that sound like him, plus a recruiting film to help him grow his team at Legacy Realty Partners.

The capture

We built Danny's likeness from three looks he approved, refined into five clean, studio-grade references with true skin texture — including two mid-speech frames for natural lip sync. For the recruiting film we used photographs of his actual Legacy Realty office as empty sets and placed his digital twin inside them, lit by the room's own light.

The craft

Each film is written to one listener. Probate Outreach speaks to the executor settling an estate: quiet and respectful, with no grief clichés. Agent Recruiting moves through his office in a series of cinematic setups. Every revision note — down to how the Legacy sign sits on his desk — was resolved before delivery, and two captioned verticals carry the same voice to social.

Dental Arts San Diego · practice filmFilmed entirely in her own office

The client

Dr. Alena Nawrocki has cared for families in El Cajon for more than 25 years, with a team that speaks English, Russian, Spanish, Tagalog and Japanese. Her son Ron, a dental student, is preparing to join the practice. They wanted video that feels like walking through their front door.

The capture

We captured everything on site, at Dental Arts San Diego: reference photographs of Alena and Ron from every angle, their voices for cloning, and the practice itself — the hallway lined with her portraits, the reception desk, the operatories. One session produced two complete likeness sets and a library of real-location plates.

The craft

Every environment in these films is a real photograph of her office, relit — never a fabricated room. We tuned the look to her own notes: bright, clean and professional, true to her age and skin, never smoothed. And we never show a procedure, only the care and the result. The practice film led to nine short spots — toothaches, emergencies, implants, dentures, aligners — and vertical cuts for social.

The Touch

Extreme macro: the balm tip meets her lower lip at a quarter speed and the gloss blooms. Under it, a whispered hush.

The brief

A South African agency asked us to help pitch Softlips Repair Cherry to the brand. We built the campaign concept, the storyboard, the model and a talking-avatar idea that could greet shoppers and send them straight to checkout.

The concept

The idea is a whisper: a hush, a touch of balm, a knowing smile — and one question, "So… what do your lips say about you?" Seven shots in fifteen seconds, from an extreme-macro touch to the product hero and end card.

The craft

The model is an original character, designed from the agency's concept references, so the brand would own a face no one else can use. Each frame was art-directed for skin, gloss and light, then tested in motion — the hush below is one of those tests.

The brief

PLAUD makes AI voice recorders. Their creator agency sent a brief in French: a native-feeling TikTok ad for the NotePro, the product named in the first ten seconds, and no hint of secret recording. We answered the same day with a finished spec ad.

The creator

She's an original AI persona — warm, early thirties, the coworker you'd trust over coffee — designed for this brief and consistent from shot to shot, from the selfie open to the hand-held product close-ups.
