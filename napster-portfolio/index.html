03What it's done for the business

  • The companion graduated from feature to revenue architecture. Our coaching program sells 30-day companion access as a core paid deliverable; our premium service tiers are anchored on it.
  • Twin work is paid work — real clients, real invoices. And the companion is what keeps those clients coming back: a video project ends at delivery, but a companion stays live on the client's site — so it needs fresh content, new campaigns, updates. One-time projects become ongoing relationships.
  • The lead-capture flow is our most reliable "wow." In live demos, watching a visitor question become an instantly emailed lead is the moment prospects lean in — every time.

We said it plainly on our call: commercial traction is 60–90 days old, so what we're showing is breadth and product shape, not volume curves. The volume story is what the partnership is for.

04Field learnings for the partner ecosystem

  • Still-image companions are a sellable tier today. Voice plus identity does the conversion work; clients don't wait for video to buy. Position it as a product, not a placeholder.
  • Source-image prep is the #1 onboarding friction. Companions need 16:9 landscape source images, but brands arrive with vertical headshots and square logos — so the first build stalls before it starts. And how you crop matters: framing and hair placement have to stay consistent or the avatar's likeness drifts between regenerations. A short asset-prep guide — required dimensions, framing rules, what to shoot — would save every new partner that first wasted cycle.
  • You can't sell a companion you can't switch off. We never put API credentials in a client's web page. Each client's avatar connects through its own small server-side endpoint that issues a fresh session token on request. That one pattern is what turns a companion into a sellable service: access is scoped to that client alone, it can be revoked the day a contract ends, and sessions can be counted for billing. A published reference implementation would let every agency partner sell companions on day one instead of inventing this themselves.
  • Time-boxed access sells. "30 days of the coach" creates urgency, caps cost, and builds re-up demand — packaging guidance like this belongs in partner enablement.

05Exhibit: the 84-finding integration report — how we partner

Between June and July we took the Companion API from first contact to a production fleet — and we documented every edge we hit along the way. The result is a formal report delivered to the Napster Developer & Product Team: Integration Findings v2 — 84 documented findings, superseding our 26-finding June report, covering the full Azure Marketplace subscription workflow and every layer of the Companion integration: embedding, sessions and webhooks, tool calls, fleet-scale rebuilds, and a complete account outage.

What the documentation discipline looks like:

  • Every finding tagged — [OPEN] / [RESOLVED] / [NEW] / [CORRECTED] — and severity-rated by real blast radius: CRITICAL (blocked deployment), HIGH (developer-experience cost), MEDIUM (polish)
  • Self-correcting: where our June report got something wrong, v2 says so prominently — so errors never propagate into Napster's docs or support scripts
  • Credit where due: resolved items are marked and thanked — this is partnership documentation, not a complaint ledger
  • Written for the next partner: "every item below cost us time that the next partner shouldn't have to spend" — which is partner enablement, done from the partner side
