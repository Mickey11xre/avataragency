Azure Marketplace × Napster Companion:
Subscription-Expiration Postmortem

Michael Rivera, AvatarAgency · Incident of July 17–18, 2026 · Shared with the Napster team in the spirit of making onboarding smoother for future partners

Why this memo is on this page: it's a working sample of how we partner. When our production agents went down to a billing-integration edge case, we didn't just get reactivated — we documented the root causes and sent Napster a prioritized set of product recommendations, most of which live on the Azure-native side of the integration. This is the enablement instinct, applied.

Setup context

Our payment method exists only at the Azure account level (entered at Azure signup, standard). We never entered payment info with Napster — everything bills through Azure Marketplace. So the only billing surface we ever watched said "Active, billing normally" throughout the outage.

Root cause of the confusion — three compounding assumptions

  1. The plan label. The 6/16 purchase was labeled "Pay-As-You-Go, $0 upfront," which reads as open-ended metered billing. In reality it created a 1-month term subscription with auto-renew defaulted OFF — the opposite of how virtually every other SaaS product behaves.
  2. The free-trial credits. Azure's signup grants $200 in credits, and the natural assumption is that billing only begins once those credits are exhausted. It's not made clear that the $200 is use-it-or-lose-it within 30 days and that the trial itself expires on a clock, independent of how much credit remains. We planned around a balance; the system was running a countdown.
  3. The warnings that didn't land. Digging back through Gmail afterward, Azure did send expiration notices. But they arrived in a personal inbox, styled like generic Azure marketing/billing mail, and nothing in them said what actually mattered: "your Napster Companion subscription will terminate and your production AI agents will stop working on this date." A warning that doesn't name the consequence isn't a warning.

What made recovery hard

  1. The Azure subscription and the Napster Companion API subscription are different objects — and the visible one read "Active, billing normally" the whole time.
  2. An expired Napster API subscription is terminal — no reactivate button, repurchase only. This is the part that felt most broken: the org, the agents, the configuration all still existed, but the system's answer was "open a new account." Expiration should be forgiving — pay again, flip the switch, same account turns back on.
  3. Our repurchase completed cleanly on Azure's side ("configured successfully") but never mapped back to our existing org — the publisher-side gap the Napster team manually bridged ~24 hours later.

Suggestions, in priority order

  1. Auto-renew ON by default — the single fix that would have prevented the whole incident, and it matches user expectations from every other subscription product.
  2. Consequence-specific warnings from the Napster side at T-7 and T-1: "you will be billed $X on [date] / your agents stop on [date]" — Napster knows an org is about to die even when Azure's notices read as noise.
  3. Clear trial framing at signup: credits expire with the trial window; the $200 is not a balance that gates billing.
  4. Forgiving expiration: reactivate-in-place for a lapsed subscription instead of terminal cancellation + repurchase + manual remapping.
  5. Dashboard banner that distinguishes "your Napster Companion API subscription expired on [date]" from a generic "inactive."

Smaller onboarding smoothers for productization: the invisible-until-enabled preview offer, the Microsoft.SaaS resource-provider prerequisite, and the unlabeled activation-propagation window.

We recognize some of this lives on Microsoft's side of the Azure-native integration rather than Napster's — but as a partner, the experience arrives as one product, so we shared the whole picture. And genuinely — thank you to the Napster team for the fast manual reactivation and the demo credits.
