P1 · Twenty Minutes

Ready for your review
AudienceSingle-site owners and managers: shopping centers, offices, churches, HOAs
Length86 words · about 34 seconds

Why it works: Opens on the finished shot and the audience callout, then pays it off with the process footage that earns the most views in this niche.

Script · spoken by your avatar (A-roll)
Visuals · A-roll and B-roll
HookProperty owners: see that pothole by your entrance? Give my crew twenty minutes.
A-ROLL: Andre's avatar in a parking lot, the wrapped Pothole Heroes truck behind him. He points off camera. Quick cut to the pothole, then to the same spot finished and smooth. On-screen timer: 20:00.
HowHere's how. We don't cut it out and haul it away. We heat the asphalt that's already there, rework it, add fresh mix and compact it.
B-ROLL: infrared heater lowering over the pothole and glowing. Rake working the softened asphalt. Fresh mix added. Plate compactor finishing. Timer runs in the corner. Andre in a small picture-in-picture.
ObjectionNo saw. No dump trucks blocking your lot. (No angry tenants.)
B-ROLL: wide shot of the lot staying open, cars passing the small work zone. A-ROLL: Andre delivers the aside with a half smile.
PayoffYour customers drive over it the same day.
B-ROLL: a car rolls over the finished patch. Before and after split screen.
WhoI'm Andre Neidich, and this is what we do at Pothole Heroes: patch, seal, stripe, with our own crews.
A-ROLL: Andre, medium shot, truck logo visible. Lower third: Andre Neidich, CEO, Pothole Heroes.
Call to actionTap the link for more information. Let's fix yours.
A-ROLL: Andre gestures down. End card: Pothole Heroes logo, phone number, link button.
Call to action“Tap the link for more information. Let's fix yours.”

Script P3

P3 · The Seam

Ready for your review
AudienceProperty managers who have paid for the same repair twice
Length96 words · about 38 seconds

Why it works: A contrarian teaching hook. It names a failure the viewer has seen and explains it in one picture.

Script · spoken by your avatar (A-roll)
Visuals · A-roll and B-roll
HookProperty managers: your last pothole patch didn't fail in the middle. It failed at the edge.
B-ROLL: close-up of an old square patch with cracked, open edges. A-ROLL: Andre crouched beside it, pointing at the seam.
WhyHere's why. A traditional patch gets cut out with a saw. That leaves four seams, and every seam is a door for water.
B-ROLL: simple animated diagram. A square patch, four edges highlighted in gold, water droplets entering each one.
CostWater gets in, the patch loosens, and you pay for the same hole twice.
B-ROLL: time-lapse style shot of the patch edges breaking down. A-ROLL: Andre shakes his head.
AnswerInfrared is different. We heat the old asphalt and the new into one piece. No seam. No door.
B-ROLL: infrared heater glowing, then the finished patch blending into the surrounding surface with no visible edge.
BonusIt's also the greener way, because we recycle your asphalt right on site.
B-ROLL: softened asphalt being reworked in place. Small recycling icon. No dump truck in sight.
Who and call to actionI'm Andre Neidich with Pothole Heroes. Tap the link for more information.
A-ROLL: Andre standing on the finished patch. End card with logo and link button.
Call to action“I'm Andre Neidich with Pothole Heroes. Tap the link for more information.”
Please confirm before production
  • The "four seams" explanation is on the website. Andre to confirm he is comfortable comparing methods this directly.
  • Infrared suits surface defects over a sound base. We avoid calling it a fix for every pothole.
