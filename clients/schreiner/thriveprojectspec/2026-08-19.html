Section 01

Executive summary

Your current site is a 20-page Wix build whose structure works against revenue: two of your three revenue lines — consulting and speaking — have no pages at all, the same package is priced differently in two places, and the purchase path ends in a generic contact form. The concept design you approved solves the emotional problem: it is beautiful, alive, and true to your research. This document solves the structural problem — how every part of your work plugs into that concept in a way that flows naturally to purchase.

The five structural decisions this report recommends:
  1. Product-first navigation, audience-second. Primary navigation mirrors your peer set (NSSE, CCSSE, HERI): Instruments · Packages & Pricing · Research & Results · Consulting & Speaking · About. Audience routing ("For campus leaders / For researchers / For faculty & staff") lives as homepage cards rather than as the navigation spine.
  2. One canonical pricing page with a five-package comparison table — the $10,000 Thriving Campus Package anchoring the right edge and a "Most Popular" badge on the Expanded Report — replacing the three separate pricing surfaces on the site today.
  3. Dual purchase rails on every package: Stripe Checkout for card/p-card buyers, and a quote→invoice rail (Stripe Quotes + Invoicing, PO number field, Net 30, ACH) for procurement offices. Pay first, intake after — never intake before payment.
  4. The animation is a layer, not the page. The approved scroll experience renders above a complete semantic HTML document. Text is never painted into canvas frames; a reduced-motion/no-JS version is the crawlable, accessible fallback.
  5. Answer-page layer for AEO: dedicated definitional pages (/what-is-thriving/, one page per instrument, a research library with DOIs) carry the AI-citation weight, so the cinematic homepage does not have to compromise to earn it.

Method note: the industry and e-commerce findings in Section 09 come from multi-source research across comparable vendors and published usability studies; every claim carries a source link in Appendix B. The current-site map draws on our full crawl of August 7, re-verified for this document.

Section 05

The purchase engine — Stripe dual-rail flow

One canonical rule, drawn from your true peer set (NSSE, CCSSE, HERI, Gallup Education): publish every price, let the buyer choose the rail. University p-card limits sit at $2,500–$5,000 per transaction, so instant card checkout serves the $250–$3,000 tiers; the $10,000 Thriving Campus Package converts through quote→invoice. Both rails converge on the same post-purchase intake form, which removes the back-and-forth of collecting invoicing details by email after a sale.

Stripe build decisions (recommended):
  • Stripe Checkout Sessions (hosted or embedded), not a custom Elements cart — built-in line items, receipts, discounts, custom fields, adjustable quantities/optional items handle "package + add-on instruments" without cart plumbing. A true multi-step cart is over-engineering for 12 SKUs and adds the exact friction Baymard measures (23 avg form elements vs ~12 ideal; ~35% conversion lift available from checkout design alone).
  • Stripe Quotes + Invoicing for Rail 2 — Stripe documents PO numbers as the canonical custom-field use; Net-30 default terms in the invoice template; ACH enabled.
  • Skip Stripe Tax at launch. Electronically delivered survey access + reports from a California sole proprietor are not taxable in CA (CDTFA Pub. 109). Enable Stripe's free threshold monitoring; keep every deliverable digital — bundling anything physical (e.g., printed reports) would make the whole sale taxable.
  • Collect the PO up front in the request form — Stripe custom fields work in Checkout but can't be added on the hosted invoice page at pay time.
  • Publish refund/withdrawal terms near checkout (NSSE publishes theirs; it reads as institutional confidence, and your existing refund-policy content migrates here).

The procurement kit — /procurement/ NEW

Universities cannot pay a new vendor without onboarding paperwork — a signed W-9 plus a supporting quote, which takes 5–7 business days at a typical institution. A single page carrying a downloadable W-9, a vendor information sheet (EIN, remit-to address), a sole-source justification template, and a sample quote and invoice removes days from every purchase-order sale. None of the comparable vendors we examined offer this.

Section 07

AEO + SEO structural requirements — baked in from day one

The single most important technical fact for this build: none of the major AI crawlers execute JavaScript (Vercel/MERJ server-log study — GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot all fetch JS files but process them as text). A canvas-driven homepage that assembles its content in JavaScript is an empty page to every AI system we want recommending your work. The fix is architectural, and it protects the animation rather than limiting it.

The two-layer homepage

LayerWhat it isWho consumes it
Presentation layerThe approved scroll experience: canvas frame-scrub of the growth sequence, animated logo, butterfly, film. Loads after first paint and drives from native scroll position (no wheel-event hijacking — preserves anchor links and in-page find).Human visitors with JS + motion enabled
Semantic layerA complete HTML document in the initial server response: H1, the thriving definition verbatim, section copy as real DOM text nodes (never pixels painted into canvas frames), nav links, footer, JSON-LD. The narrative text that appears between animation beats IS this DOM text, positioned and revealed by CSS.Every crawler, every AI system, screen readers, and the prefers-reduced-motion / no-JS fallback (static final-tree hero image, all content visible)

Verification rule for the whole build: curl any page — what curl sees is what GPTBot sees. If the content isn't in the curl output, it doesn't exist. This runs as an automated check on every deploy, not a launch-day check — curl the homepage and assert the thriving definition, all seven movement headings, nav links and JSON-LD are present.

Why the answer pages carry the AEO weight

  • The unit of AI citation is the chunk, not the page — an H2 plus the content beneath it, independently answerable. Question-formatted H2s ("What is the Thriving Quotient?") matching real query phrasing, one question per section.
  • Answer capsules: a 30–60 word direct answer directly under the H1/H2 — present in 72.4% of ChatGPT-cited posts (strongest single predictor; Search Engine Land 7,500-referral study). Keep links OUT of the capsule (~91% of cited capsules contained zero links).
  • Original data wins: 52.2% of cited posts contain proprietary data. Your national norms, sample sizes, and reliability coefficients are exactly this kind of asset — formatted as self-contained attributed stats ("In Schreiner's 2012 study of 14,067 undergraduates…"). The Princeton GEO experiment measured statistics +41%, citations up to +115%, expert quotes +28% visibility.
  • You coined the term. "Thriving Quotient" is your intellectual property, backed by DOI-linked publications. The site's job is to be the canonical source that AI systems resolve the term to. /what-is-thriving/ and the research library with ScholarlyArticle markup make that claim machine-readable.
  • Professional-services sites currently have the lowest AI citation rates of any category (<4%) — which means structured effort now, in this specific niche, is a durable advantage that is difficult for others to catch up on later.

Section 08

What this architecture cannot fix — the off-site track

Everything in Sections 01–07 happens on your own website, and all of it is necessary. But we want to be straightforward with you about a limit: the largest single component of your AI visibility score — Discovery, currently 9 out of 100 and weighted at 40% — is mostly determined by what appears on other people’s websites. This section exists so that the architecture is never mistaken for a complete solution to the problem the audit identified.

What the numbers suggest. Applying the audit’s own weighting, building this architecture exactly as specified should move your composite score from 32 to roughly 49 — a substantial gain, but still inside the "Emerging" band. Reaching "Competitive" (around 60) requires the architecture and the off-site work described below. These are estimates rather than promises, but the shape is reliable: website readiness is capped at 20% of the score, which makes it the cheapest component to maximize and the least valuable to maximize on its own. The honest summary is that the website is the necessary half, not the sufficient half.

Why your own HTML cannot solve it

Two findings from the audit make this concrete, and neither is a defect in your website:

  • When AI assistants were asked about instruments measuring student thriving, several cited Georgia Southern and Rutgers repositories — institutions hosting adaptations of your work — rather than thrivingincollege.org. They knew the instrument; they did not know where to license it.
  • On queries about retention surveys and student-success consultants, RNL is named first and you are absent — despite your co-authorship of the Student Satisfaction Inventory that underpins part of their offering.

Both are third-party citation problems. No amount of structure on your own site changes what another institution’s repository page says.

The off-site workstream (separate scope)

This is not included in the current agreement and would be scoped separately. We are naming it now so it is visible in the plan rather than surfacing as a surprise in November.

  • Third-party citation placement: getting your instruments listed — with prices and a link — on the repositories, directories, and comparison round-ups AI assistants already draw from; and pursuing the round-ups where NSSE, CCSSE and RNL appear and you do not.
  • Wikidata entity: a Wikidata item for "Thriving Quotient" naming you as its creator, linked to your researcher profile. This is how AI systems resolve a term to a specific thing rather than a string of words, and for a researcher whose coined term is the product, it is the highest-value identifier available.
