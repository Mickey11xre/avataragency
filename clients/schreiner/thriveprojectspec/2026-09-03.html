Your new website — the plan, and what we need from you

How thrivingincollege.org is being rebuilt for September 2026: what launches on the 15th, what follows after, how people will find it and buy from it — and the handful of decisions only you can make.

Prepared by: Michael Rivera & Stephanie Geter September 2026 Launch: September 15, 2026

Dr. Schreiner — before designing anything, we read every page currently published on your site. All thirty-three of them, including several that are live but no longer reachable from your menu. That reading is what this document is built on.

Fifteen years of work has accumulated on that site, and in a few places it now says two different things — a price that appears one way on one page and another way in the store, a reliability figure that differs between two pages describing the same instrument. None of this is unusual, and none of it is a criticism. It simply means that in a handful of places, only you know which version is right.

So this document does two things. The first half describes what we are building and when. The second half — What we need from you — is a short set of questions and materials. Short answers are perfectly fine: a number, a "yes," or "use the second one" is usually all we need. We'll walk through them with you together; nothing here needs answering in advance.

The one-sentence version. Your research, your instruments and your reputation are the strongest assets in this field, and your current site makes people work to reach them. The September launch rebuilds everything you have today — properly, consistently, and with a working store — and the pages that go after new audiences follow immediately after.

Section 01

What launches in September, and what follows

This is the most important thing to be clear about, so it comes first.

The September 15 launch rebuilds every page you have today. New design, correct URLs, one set of prices, a working store that delivers what people buy, and — for the first time — the underlying markup that lets search engines and AI assistants read your site properly. That last item is not cosmetic; we'll come back to it in Section 07, because it is the single largest improvement available to you and it requires no new writing from you at all.

A second set of pages follows immediately after. These are pages that don't exist on your site today and that go after audiences you currently don't reach: pages organised around the problems campuses are trying to solve, comparisons with the instruments buyers already know, your consulting and speaking work, your norming data, and a proper licensing page for researchers.

Why in that order. Two reasons, and both work in your favour.

The first is timing. This is your buying season. A live, well-presented store in the middle of it is worth more than a perfect one in October — so the work that lets a campus find you, trust you and pay you comes first.

The second is that the new pages need you. A page about measuring retention, or one comparing your instrument with NSSE, has to be written from your research and in your voice — and written well enough to be quoted. Rushing those into twelve days would produce thin pages with your name on them, which is worse than not having them yet. They deserve their own run at it, and they'll get one.

One consequence worth naming rather than discovering later: your consulting and speaking pages are in that second group. They don't exist on your site today, and they are two of the three ways you earn — so they matter, and they are coming. They are not part of the September 15 launch.

Section 02

What we're building

The design you approved — the seed that becomes a rooted tree as you scroll, the greens, the butterfly, the film above the fold — is the right homepage. What it needs beneath it is a site: a page for every instrument, one clear place to see prices, and a store that works.

Five decisions shape the September build.

  1. Navigation organised around your products. Instruments · Packages & Pricing · Thriving Knowledge Center · About, plus a visible Store. This is how the instrument providers your buyers already know are organised. The menu is also built so the problem-based pages can be added later without rearranging anything.
  2. One pricing page and one store. Every package on one comparison table, and a single storefront reached from the main menu — with a "Buy" button in the same position on every product page that jumps straight to that product. One place to look, one place to update.
  3. Two ways to pay, on every package. A card checkout for campuses buying on a purchasing card, and a quote-and-invoice path with a PO number, Net 30 terms and bank transfer for the ones that must go through procurement. Your peers offer one or the other; offering both is a real advantage.
  4. Delivery happens by itself. Where an item can be sent instantly, the buyer receives it the moment payment clears — and you are not in that loop. Where an item is custom, the store collects what you need to begin.
  5. Every fact stated plainly enough to be quoted. What each instrument measures, what it costs, how long it takes, how to license it — each written as a short, literal sentence rather than left implied. Section 07 explains why this matters more than it sounds.

One note on approach: the instrument providers closest to your work — NSSE at Indiana, CCSSE at UT Austin, Gallup's education programme — all publish their prices openly and let institutions register themselves. The consultancies hide pricing behind a sales conversation. You have fixed, published prices from $250 to $10,000, which puts you firmly in the first group, and the new site makes that transparency an advantage rather than an accident.

Section 05

How people will buy — and what happens next

Two things change. Campuses will be able to complete a purchase whichever way their institution pays; and once they have paid, the site — not you — takes care of what happens next wherever that's possible.

Two ways to pay

University purchasing cards typically cap at $2,500 to $5,000 per transaction, which covers every package except the Thriving Campus. So the site offers both paths on every package, and the buyer chooses.

By card
For a department buying on a purchasing card: a short checkout, the package plus any add-ons, and a receipt. Minutes, not weeks.
By quote and invoice
For procurement: a formal quote with a reference number for their budget packet, converted to an invoice carrying their PO number, Net 30, payable by card or bank transfer.
Both arrive in the same place
Whichever path a campus takes, delivery and set-up work identically afterwards. One pipeline, not two.

What happens after payment

This is the part of your current site that is missing, and it's where the rebuild gives you back the most time. Your products fall into three groups, and the store will know which group each item belongs to.

TypeExamplesWhat the buyer receives, and when

What will be true on September 15. The store is live, fully branded, and taking orders: one storefront, a buy button on every product, both payment paths, and automatic delivery for everything that can be delivered automatically. The more sophisticated part — where the questions a buyer is asked change depending on which package they bought, and the custom-work intake runs itself — lands shortly after launch. We'd rather tell you that now than have it be a surprise later.

One practical matter: the Stripe account

The new store runs on Stripe, and the account has to be opened by you, in your name — Stripe verifies the legal entity that receives the money, which is not something that can be delegated, and shouldn't be. An account opened under our name would route your revenue through our business, which neither of us wants. It takes about fifteen minutes and needs your business details and bank information.

Once it exists, you add us as a team member from your dashboard so we can build the store inside it. Worth saying plainly: you never share a password, the invitation is individually revocable at any moment, every action any team member takes is recorded in your security history, and ownership, payouts and the ability to close the account remain yours alone.

We'll also need to agree what happens to PayPal during the change-over — whether it stays live while the new store beds in, and who watches anything in flight.

Section 06

The Thriving Knowledge Center

Your publications, presentations and doctoral studies are, collectively, something almost no instrument in this field can match. At the moment they exist as three lists ordered by date — which answers only one question, "what is newest," and answers it least well of all.

The Knowledge Center reorganises the same material around the question people actually arrive with: what does the research say about students like mine?

PrincipleWhat it means in practice
Learner first, year secondThe top level is the population — sophomores, adult learners, graduate students, community college, faculty and staff, whole institutions. Inside a category you can filter by year, so a researcher looking for pandemic-era work, or for the earliest studies, still arrives in two clicks. Whether an item is a publication, a presentation or a dissertation becomes a filter rather than its address.
One home for the depthReliability figures, factor analyses, sample sizes and methodology live here. The instrument pages carry one clear line of evidence and a link — so someone deciding whether to survey their campus isn't reading psychometrics, and someone who wants the psychometrics finds all of it in one place.
Everything findable, obtained properlyEach item says where it is obtained. Where the work is yours to share, we host it. Where it belongs to its author or a publisher — several of the dissertations, and articles published in journals — we link to the place it is properly bought or downloaded. Items with no available file are still listed in full, with their DOI where one exists, so a reader can always find them even when they can't download them here.
An invitation, not a filing cabinetA visitor should be able to see the shape of fifteen years of work — how much, on whom, showing what — and follow their own interest into it.
Two things that make this section stronger than it looks. First, the version we most want to build comes next: a way of seeing what this body of research has shown — the scale, the populations, the findings — that a provost absorbs in thirty seconds and a researcher digs into for an hour. That is worth doing well rather than quickly, so at launch the Knowledge Center is the clean, categorised library described above.

Second, and more immediately useful: your twenty doctoral dissertations are independent evidence. When we asked AI assistants about your instrument, two of them noted that the validation appears to come primarily from the developers' own studies. Those dissertations are the answer — other researchers, at other institutions, using your instrument and publishing what they found. Organised by population rather than buried in a date-ordered list, they stop being a bibliography and start being third-party validation.

Section 07

Being found — by search engines, and by AI assistants

Something has changed in how institutional buyers begin their search, and it affects you more than most. Increasingly the first question isn't typed into Google — it's asked of an AI assistant. "How should we measure student thriving?" "What's an alternative to NSSE?" The answer shapes the shortlist before anyone visits a website at all.

We tested this properly rather than assuming. Twenty-two questions a buyer might ask, put to five different AI assistants — a hundred and six answers in all. You were named in a small minority of them. Where the questions used your own vocabulary you sometimes appeared; where they used the words a provost would actually use — retention, belonging, engagement, student success — you mostly didn't.

Two answers from the same assistant on the same afternoon explain the whole problem, and the whole fix.

The questionWhat happened
"How much does a campus-wide student success survey cost?"It opened your pricing page, unprompted, read out your price card, and listed you alongside NSSE and CCSSE. It worked because the fact was published as plain, readable text.
"How do I get permission to use the Thriving Quotient in my dissertation?"It read ninety-five sources, opened your own surveys page, couldn't find who to ask, and sent the researcher to a university faculty directory instead. It failed because that fact exists only inside a downloadable form.
The rule that follows, and it governs every page we build: every fact a buyer might act on has to exist on your site as a short, literal, quotable sentence. Not implied. Not inside a PDF. Not expressed only as prose about research.

What the September build does about it

What we're doingWhy
Adding the machine-readable layer your site has never had.Your site currently carries none of the behind-the-scenes markup that tells a search engine or an assistant what it's looking at. Five priced products are published with no machine-readable name, price or availability — which is a large part of why assistants describe your instruments as questions to borrow rather than a product to license. This is the single biggest improvement available to you, and it needs no new writing from you at all.
A real question-and-answer section on every instrument page.Six to ten genuine questions with direct answers — what it measures, how long it takes, how it was validated, what a campus receives, what it costs, how to license it. Q&A is the format assistants quote most readily, and no page on your site currently uses it.
Making every word readable without JavaScript.The systems that read the web don't watch animations or run the code that draws them. Your current site already passes this test — we're protecting an existing strength rather than adding one, which is why the animation sits on top of a complete document rather than replacing it.
One price, everywhere, in plain text.An assistant that finds two different prices for the same product on the same site will trust neither. This is also why the pricing question below matters more than it might appear.
Two honest notes. The first: the pages that go after the buyer's own vocabulary — retention, belonging, the sophomore slump — are in the group that follows launch, so the September work will move some of these measures a great deal and others only a little. We'll re-run the same twenty-two questions in November and show you exactly what moved.

The second: a great website is the necessary half of this, not the whole of it. Some of what determines whether assistants name you happens elsewhere — in the repositories and comparison articles they already rely on, several of which currently point at other institutions' copies of your instruments rather than at you. That's separate work, and we'd rather say so plainly now than let a beautiful site carry an expectation it can't meet alone.
