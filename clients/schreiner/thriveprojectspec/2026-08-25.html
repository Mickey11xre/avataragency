Section 01

Executive summary

Your current site is a 33-page Wix build whose structure works against revenue: two of your three revenue lines — consulting and speaking — have no pages at all, the same package is priced differently in two places, and the purchase path ends in a generic contact form. The concept design you approved solves the emotional problem: it is beautiful, alive, and true to your research. This document solves the structural problem — how every part of your work plugs into that concept in a way that flows naturally to purchase.

The five structural decisions this report recommends:
  1. Product-first navigation, audience-second. Primary navigation mirrors your peer set (NSSE, CCSSE, HERI): Instruments · Packages & Pricing · Research & Results · Consulting & Speaking · About. Audience routing ("For campus leaders / For researchers / For faculty & staff") lives as homepage cards rather than as the navigation spine.
  2. One canonical pricing page with a five-package comparison table — the $10,000 Thriving Campus Package anchoring the right edge and a "Most Popular" badge on the Expanded Report — replacing the three separate pricing surfaces on the site today.
  3. Dual purchase rails on every package: Stripe Checkout for card/p-card buyers, and a quote→invoice rail (Stripe Quotes + Invoicing, PO number field, Net 30, ACH) for procurement offices. Pay first, intake after — never intake before payment.
  4. The animation is a layer, not the page. The approved scroll experience renders above a complete semantic HTML document. Text is never painted into canvas frames; a reduced-motion/no-JS version is the crawlable, accessible fallback.
  5. Answer-page layer for AEO: dedicated definitional pages (/what-is-thriving/, one page per instrument, a research library with DOIs) carry the AI-citation weight, so the cinematic homepage does not have to compromise to earn it.

Method note: the industry and e-commerce findings in Section 09 come from multi-source research across comparable vendors and published usability studies; every claim carries a source link in Appendix B. The current-site map draws on our full crawl of August 7, re-verified for this document.

Where the current structure loses buyers. The path to purchase stops short in several places: the "Dr. Laurie Schreiner" navigation item links back to the homepage; "Register Your Campus" opens directly into a store with many options and no guidance on choosing among them; pricing differs between the packages pages and the store ($2,500 vs $3,000) at the exact moment of decision; the researcher path requires downloading, printing and returning a PDF; and consulting and speaking — the two highest-value services — have no pages at all. Each of these is a structural issue rather than a wording one, which is why the rebuild starts here.

Three structural gaps in the concept — expected, since it was built to show the design direction rather than the site structure: (1) no navigation header — a scroll story has no way to jump to pricing, research, or contact; the build adds a persistent slim nav that appears after the first movement. (2) No pages beneath it — every "Learn more" currently has nowhere to go. (3) No purchase mechanics — "Register your campus" must land somewhere that takes money. What is preserved untouched: the scroll-driven seedling→roots→tree growth sequence, the green rooted palette, the butterfly accent, the animated logo, the cinematic pacing, and the film above the fold. Twenty-eight Phase 1 pages in six sections, with Phase 2/3 expansion slots (faded) already positioned so later modules plug in without restructuring nav or URLs. Homepage anatomy — the animated layer, movement by movement

The concept's five cards were visual proof, not architecture. The production homepage is seven scroll movements: a cinematic hero, one movement for each of the five site sections, and a closing router. Every movement is a beat in the growth animation and a card that links into its section — so the homepage becomes a narrative table of contents for the whole site. One deliberate reorder from the concept: evidence comes before the ask (Research & Results moves ahead of Packages), because for a $1,500–$10,000 institutional purchase, proof must precede price.
