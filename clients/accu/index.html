The client

Paul Small is a native Angeleno, a former talent agent and a Certified Senior Advisor with Compass. He didn't want another listing video. He wanted a film that sells the neighborhood itself, the way a local would show it to a friend.

The capture

It started with one cloning session: 47 reference photographs of Paul from every angle — full body, close-ups, three-quarter and mid-speech — and his own voice. From that set we built a private likeness model, so Paul could walk the Sunday farmers market, the back patio at Alana's Coffee and Mar Vista's tree-lined streets without a camera crew in sight.

The craft

The film moves through Mar Vista the way Paul describes it: the hillside streets with ocean views, the tucked-away tree-lined blocks and the deep lots that give people breathing room; bungalows beside mid-century ranch homes and new builds; and the community at the farmers market, Alana's Coffee and Ocean View Farms. His on-camera lines are lip-synced to his own narration, then cut, graded and mixed in a professional edit.

Her knowledge

Ava was built from ACCU's own website: every loan in the menu, written into 26 knowledge files and 50 must-know answers. The core facts, who can join, the phone line and hours, and how to apply, sit in her instructions, so she states them the same way every time.

Her guardrails

She never quotes a rate, a payment or a fee, and never tells a member they are approved. She never asks for a Social Security number, an account number or income. She says she is an AI whenever it matters, and anything she doesn't know goes to a loan officer.

The handoff

Every conversation ends in a next step: the loan's Apply Online page, a call back from a loan officer, or contact details for the lending team. In a pilot, the panel connects to ACCU's own scheduling and lead systems, so the lending team gets every request.
