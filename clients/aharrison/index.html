What changed in Version 2 — and why: this is the style you chose on September 4th, drawn for the first time. The pencil-drawn “duck book” look from Adele’s references, in the Jan Brett family — deliberately not another AI picture book, but something meant to read as a classic in twenty years.

The rule underneath it: only the characters are in colour. Everything around them — the rooms, the props, the people — stays in warm sepia and greyscale. That came from Bill’s observation that dogs don’t see in colour, which turns a style decision into part of the story: the monochrome world is how Lady Belle sees it. It is also why humans stay knees-down and hands only, so that, in Adele’s words, every child can put themselves in that family.

One consequence worth knowing: a white dog in a sepia world has no colour of her own, so Belle’s red collar and gold “Kindness” tag are what mark her as the colour character. Bill’s collar idea turned out to be structural, not decorative. Belle now sits on her bed in the family room with the happier, open-mouth expression from the original concept; the cats are shown as solo seated portraits, both squared to camera, so each gets as much detail as she does. Shadow’s eyes are yellow and Butterball’s are green, per your canon, and Butterball has his stubby bobbed tail.

These are not two competing directions. Version 1 is the June concept art, and the characters in it are approved — that has not changed. What changed is the rendering: Version 1 is watercolour, which predates the style you settled on. It stays here so you can see that Belle, Shadow and Butterball came through the restyle as themselves.

What we need from you: does each animal still look like your animal in the new style? Coat, markings, expression, and their size next to one another. And two specific things we already have questions about — is Butterball grumpy enough, and Bill, your photos of the 25-pound cat would settle his build for good if you can dig them out. Notes now are far cheaper than notes after the interior pages are drawn.

The concept: Lady Belle at the centre, ringed by the nine fruits of the Spirit, with Fruit of the Spirit as the series line beneath the title. She wears the same red collar and “Kindness” tag as the character art, so the book’s first virtue is carried in the mark itself.

Two versions, one decision. Belle and the fruit are identical in both — the same drawing, in the same places. The only thing that changes is what holds the ring together. Version one uses the pale blue ribbon weaving between the fruits. Version two takes the ribbon away and replaces it with warm light coming from behind her, so the fruits sit in the glow rather than on a ribbon. Belle herself stays white in both. Which one do you want to take forward?

What to look at: the fruits are the ring, so their choice and order are worth a careful look — whether these are the nine you want, and whether any of them should change. Then the type: the title face, the weight of Fruit of the Spirit beneath it, and whether Belle reads clearly when this is printed small on a spine or a website header.

One thing we deliberately watched: Bill raised the risk that a circular ribbon could read as another well-known logo. In version one the ribbon is drawn soft and broken, weaving behind separate pieces of fruit rather than as a continuous swirling emblem. Version two removes the ribbon altogether, which takes that reading away — but it introduces light instead, and light carries its own associations. Say plainly which of the two sits more comfortably with you. It is far easier to change here than after it is on a cover.
