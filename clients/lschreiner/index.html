In its first month the new site did what it was built to do: it sold surveys on its own. Five universities paid online by card, without anyone needing to send an invoice, and three more asked for an invoice through the new form. Together that is $36,600 in orders and requests.

Most visitors still arrive by typing the address or clicking a link in an email, which is normal for a site that just launched. Google search is growing into the second big source. AI assistants, mostly ChatGPT, are already recommending the site and sending people straight to the instrument pages.

4 · AI referrals

Are AI assistants recommending you?

Yes, and earlier than expected. ChatGPT and Gemini sent 42 visits in the first month. That is 3% of all visits, about three times the average for websites today.

Why this matters

More and more people ask an AI assistant instead of searching Google: "How do I measure whether our students are thriving?" The assistant answers in a paragraph and, if it trusts a website, links to it. In our August review of the old site, AI tools often described your ideas but rarely linked to you or knew the surveys could be purchased. Your AI visibility score was 32 out of 100.

How an AI recommendation becomes a visit

What we built into the new site for AI

  • A guide written for AI toolsA plain summary file that tells AI what the seven instruments are, what each package costs and how to order.
  • Headings phrased as questions"What does it mean to thrive in college?" matches the way people ask AI, so it can quote your answer.
  • Questions and answers on every instrumentCost, sample size, IRB and permissions, answered right on each page.
  • One price list everywhereThe old site showed three different prices. AI now sees one consistent answer.
  • Comparison pagesThriving Quotient vs. NSSE, and thriving vs. engagement, retention and belonging, for people who search using other words.
  • AI visit trackingThe site now records every visit that arrives from an AI tool, which is how we can show you this section.

Which AI sent visitors

Our honest read

The early signs are good: AI tools are finding the new site, trusting it and linking to the right pages. The numbers are still small, so one month is too soon to call it a trend. We could not yet link any of the six online sales to an AI visit, because Google's tracking misses most purchases (see next steps). In mid-November we will repeat the August test, asking the same 22 questions to five AI assistants, and give you a new score to compare against the 32 from August.
